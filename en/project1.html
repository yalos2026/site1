Chemical thermal indicators are divided into 2 types: Type 1 - reversible thermal indicators; Type 2 - irreversible thermal indicators. The distinctive feature between them is the fact that during equipment overheating, irreversible thermal indicators after their activation by changing color, under conditions of temperature drop below the threshold value, do not return to their original color. While reversible thermal indicators return to their original color. Both types of thermal indicators have their own advantages and areas of application. Irreversible thermal indicators are used where maintenance personnel must know exactly whether there was heating to the controlled temperature on the equipment surface during their absence, and reversible thermal indicators are used where, at the time of personnel inspection, it is possible to visually see the current temperature on the controlled surface of the equipment under examination. Thermal indicators enhance the effectiveness of the visual method for detecting overheating of power and thermal equipment.

Comparison with the use of thermal imagers and digital thermometers Thermal indicators occupy their niche in the means of controlling temperature overheating of thermal and power equipment, however, they do not replace various electronic safety systems, including they do not replace the use of thermal imagers and digital thermometers, which have their own application limitations. It is also important that thermal imagers and digital thermometers used allow you to see only the current temperature of objects.
Limitations and conditions for using Thermal Imagers "Instructions for thermal control and diagnostics" - the thermal imaging method of temperature control is used to detect and identify heating areas; - temperature control by the thermal imaging method is recommended to be carried out on outdoor surfaces not exposed to solar radiation for the preceding 12 hours; - temperature control by the thermal imaging method is carried out on devices located outdoors. However, it is not recommended to carry out in wet weather, namely: in rain, fog, snowfall, in the presence of frost, and any other moisture. Also, this method of temperature control limits the wind speed, which should not exceed 7 m/s and the air temperature, which should be within the operating temperature range of the thermal imager; - temperature control by the thermal imaging method is not recommended on surfaces with an emissivity coefficient below 0.7; - when carrying out temperature control on objects with an emissivity coefficient below 0.7, the surface of the object is treated with special means of painting, blackening, oxidation, etc., while the ambient air temperature at the workplace should be in the range from + 5 C to + 30 C; - when carrying out temperature control in the winter period, the device must be transported and stored in a special thermos case with heating; - the operating time of the thermal imager should not exceed the value specified in its technical passport. At low temperatures, optics fogging, rapid battery discharge, and condensation inside the device occur, which negatively affects the operation and, as a result, leads to device failure; - when working with a thermal imager, it is necessary to focus on the correct choice of the emissivity coefficient of the controlled object; At the same time, for subsequent calculations of the actual characteristics of the controlled object and detected defects, calculation models should be used taking into account the features of stationary and non-stationary heat transfer processes.
Limitations and conditions for using Infrared Pyrometers To determine the exact temperature with any infrared radiation thermometer, it is necessary to have certain knowledge with the observance of the correct measurement methodology: - Before each measurement, it is necessary to control the correct choice of emissivity values on the measured surface. Measurements carried out with a different value are unreliable. - Each material has its own emissivity value (including the state of the surface of this material matters: roughness, oxidation, etc.). This fact must also be taken into account when choosing the correct emission coefficient, and if they are unknown, the measurements will be incorrect. - The indicated temperature will be incorrect if the size of the object is smaller than the field of view. - If the object whose temperature should be measured does not fill the entire field of view, the device receives radiation from other objects of the surrounding environment, thereby causing measurement error. - It is necessary to eliminate side lighting from surrounding objects or necessarily take into account its influence on the measurement result. - Measurements are made only at the time of inspection. In the event that overheating occurred before the temperature control measurement, it is impossible to establish this. - For pyrometers with a standard fixed emissivity of 0.95, the temperature of objects with an emissivity coefficient different from 0.95 must be calculated using special tables. - It is necessary to take into account the presence of measurement errors in winter operating conditions.
